# Algorithmic philosophy: Counterform

**Family:** `aup-editorial-v1-fable`  
**Lineage:** Editorial Geometry  
**Run:** Fable 5 comparison run (parallel interpretation; does not replace `aup-editorial-v1`)  
**Stage:** probes (5 of 10)  
**Brief:** `docs/designpowers/briefs/2026-08-13-illustration-algorithmic-art-handoff.md`

---

Counterform borrows its name from typography: the shape of the air inside and around a letter, which the eye reads as surely as the ink. This movement treats the empty canvas the same way. At least two fifths of every print stays untouched, and that emptiness is not leftover space, it is the counterform that gives the printed marks their meaning. Every composition hangs from a single editorial anchor, a thin rule or an implied axis, the way a well set page hangs from its baseline grid. The marks do not float; they take a position relative to that line, and the position is the idea.

The alphabet is deliberately small and never grows. Circle, capsule, rounded rectangle, triangle, thin rule, quarter-arc. Every coordinate snaps to an 8px grid. Every angle sits at or near 0, 45, or 90 degrees. Depth is achieved by one means only: transparency and overlap. When two translucent masses cross, the intersection darkens into a third tone, and that lens of shared ink is the only shading the system permits. No gradients, no shadows, no texture, no mesh. If a print needs more than this alphabet to say its idea, the idea is not yet clear enough to print.

Each print carries exactly five roles, meticulously rationed: one field mass that holds the weight, one mute companion that answers it, one to three small seeded satellites that keep the air alive, one force structure that carries the idea, and one accent mark in the category hue. The accent is the smallest voice in the room and therefore the loudest. Violet, where a category demands it, lives only on that accent mark and never becomes a field or a ground. Everything else is neutral: near-white ink on the dark editorial canvas, near-black ink on light paper, with the same geometry in both. Themes recolor; they never redraw.

Category identity is a force applied to the shared procedure, never a new style. The same placement engine runs for every category; what changes is a set of bias weights that pull the anchor, the masses, and the force structure toward one reading. A boundary category pulls the anchor into a hard permission edge with a figure held just beyond it. A steering category pulls one dominant vector across the page. A process category pulls the marks into a row of progressive closures. A multi-agent category pulls influence through overlapping translucent centers. A repair category breaks the longest line and stitches it. A reader who knows the category question should feel the force in the arrangement. Everyone else should simply meet a calm poster.

Randomness in Counterform is seeded and answerable. The seed derives from `aup-editorial-v1-fable|categoryId|hex`, and the same seed always yields the same print, exactly. Stepping the seed does not jitter the current print; it pulls a new print from the same series, the way a printmaker pulls the next sheet from a tuned plate: same plate, same ink, a fresh legal arrangement. The random stream decides only among moves the grammar already allows, which side holds the mass, how far the throw, where the break opens, how many satellites breathe in the margin. It never invents a new mark and it never breaks the snap.

The craft bar is master-level restraint: every ratio tuned, every alignment intentional, the look of countless iterations compressed into a short deterministic procedure. A Counterform print should sit beside a Laws of UX card without apology, one idea, generous air, and nothing that needs explaining away. When in doubt, the algorithm removes a mark rather than adding one. The page is finished not when there is nothing left to draw, but when the counterform reads as clearly as the ink.
