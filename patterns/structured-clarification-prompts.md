# Structured clarification prompts

> When the agent needs input, it asks with concrete options rather than open-ended questions.

## Overview

'What do you mean?' stalls the work. Structured clarification presents the ambiguity as choices (interpreted meanings, scoped options, sensible defaults) so resolving it takes one tap, not one paragraph.

## Why it works

Options reveal what the agent considered, teach its reasoning, and lower response effort to near zero. Open questions transfer the whole cognitive burden back to the user.

## When to use it

- Ambiguity with a small set of plausible readings
- Mobile and voice contexts where typing is costly
- Repeated decision points that deserve remembered defaults

## When to avoid it

- Genuinely open creative direction, where options would anchor too early

## Examples in the wild

- Multiple-choice clarifying questions in agentic coding tools
- Slot-filling chips in travel and booking flows
- Disambiguation prompts in search
